# **Annex 5: Roles in a team**

Each team splits into three roles: **Coder, Researcher** and **Communicator**, with two
people per role by default. Choose based on your profiles and interests. If your team has
no data scientist, the person most curious about coding becomes a Coder, with help from
the AI and the mentors.


Each role leads part of the task and supports the rest. Coders carry most of the load at the
start, producing the data findings the others build on. Researchers work throughout, adding
context and evidence. Communicators carry most of the load at the end, shaping how the
results are presented. **The policy brief, the “policy tool” and the AI sheet are owned**
**by the whole team**, and every role contributes to the final products. By the end of Day 1,
the whole team agrees on a draft concept of the “policy tool” and presents the idea to
everyone.
## • Coder


Suits you if you have a data science, GIS, computer science backgrounds, or you are curious
to try coding with AI. Coding experience helps, but its absence is not a big obstacle: the
Setup Guide in the annex helps to start from scratch, and there is AI and coaches to help.

### **Tasks**


  - Lead the data analysis for Q1 and Q3.

  - Check that every result is backed by the challenge data and verified.

  - Create the figures, maps and charts for the brief and the policy tool.

## • Researcher

Suits you if you have a political science, policy, or environmental background, or you like
reading reports and checking sources.

### **Tasks:**


  - Lead the desk research for Q2.

  - Put the Coders’ results in context and compare them with public sources.

  - Supply the evidence for Q4.

  - Q2 findings with a source list, and a note of what could not be verified.

### **Outcomes**

## • Communicator


Suits you if you have a communication, media, design or journalism background, or you like
writing and explaining.

### **Tasks:**


  - Lead Q4, turning the team’s evidence into a recommendation.

  - Lead the writing of the policy brief

  - Shape the “policy tool”: decide its format, look and key messages, with input from
the Coders and Researchers.

  - Collect the team’s AI notes throughout and compile the AI sheet.
